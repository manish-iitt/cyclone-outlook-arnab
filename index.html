Cyclone Arnab (Bay of Bengal): WRF outlook for Tirupati

Research simulation, HYDROS lab, IIT Tirupati. Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab, as named by IMD. Two WRF 9 km / 3 km runs: Run 1 from NOAA GFS of 25 Oct 2026, 12 UTC (17:30 IST); Run 2 from GFS of 26 Oct, 00 UTC (05:30 IST). Prepared 28 Oct 2026 for internal review.

This is not a forecast. These are model runs, not yet checked against observations, and their tracks and timing differ from what IMD reported. For warnings and decisions use the India Meteorological Department: mausam.imd.gov.in and the Amaravati / Chennai centre bulletins.

What it means for Tirupati in this run

Rain is the mean of 3 km cells within 25 km of the campus (bars) with the single campus cell in the table; wind is the highest 10 m wind within 25 km. Light rain of a few mm per day would still be possible; the model shows no heavy rain at Tirupati.

Run 1 and Run 2 compared

What IMD reported. Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh coast between 02:00 and 08:00 IST on 28 Oct, near 16.45N 81.9E (about 20 km east-northeast of Narsapur), with winds of 125 to 135 km/h, and was completely over land by 08:00 IST (IMD bulletin as reported by ANI and DT Next).

Landfall in the model. Both runs bring the centre to the same coast, but late and from the south. At 00 UTC on 28 Oct (05:30 IST, inside the IMD crossing window) the centre is about 217 km from the IMD landfall point in Run 2 and 277 km in Run 1; both stay near 14 to 15N during 27 Oct and turn north about half a day to a day too late. Run 2 is 66 km from the landfall point at 28 Oct 12 UTC (17:30 IST), about 15 km off the coast near Yanam, so its landfall falls in the hours after the run ends, roughly 10 to 15 hours after the IMD time and about 40 km to the east. Run 1 reaches the coast around 29 Oct 00 to 03 IST, about 20 to 24 hours late.

Intensity. The highest model wind (Run 2: 131 km/h, Run 1: 147 km/h) occurs on 27 Oct, before landfall, and is close to the IMD landfall wind of 125 to 135 km/h. At landfall time the models have weakened to about 105 km/h (Run 2) and 110 km/h (Run 1) at 10 m, so the model is lower than IMD at the moment of crossing.

Tirupati. Both runs keep the cyclone far from Tirupati and give almost no rain at the campus (Run 2: a few tenths of a mm in total); winds within 25 km stay below 14 m/s (50 km/h). Neither run shows heavy rain at Tirupati.

Limits: one starting field (GFS) per run, 9 km outer grid, no data assimilation, no bogus vortex. Run 2, started 18 hours later, is much better than Run 1 in timing (about 10 to 15 hours late against about a day), which shows how sensitive the result is to the starting vortex. Run 2 stops before landfall, so its landfall time is a reading of the track, not a model output. Observed IMD values are taken from news reports of the bulletins. Newer runs will be added as they finish.
